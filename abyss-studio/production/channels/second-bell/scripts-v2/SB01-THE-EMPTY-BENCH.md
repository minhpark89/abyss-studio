# SB01 · The Empty Bench

US English shooting script. Planned running time 20–22 minutes. The public-facing title is *They Asked the Stagehand to Move the Piano. She Was the Pianist on the Record.*

June Porter, 61, a Black stagehand with silver natural curls and a mustard crew shirt. Lena Park, 22, a Korean American singer with a black bob and a teal cardigan. Dean Walsh, 46, a sandy-haired venue coordinator with a mustache and a navy blazer. One community recital hall. One morning. One scholarship audition. The first five minutes are the owner review cut. [Shot and dialogue list](../../../episodes/SB01/hook-v2/qa/opening-cues.json)

## 00:00–00:44 · The piano stays

Dean looks past June's face to the brass caster on the black piano. “You're here to move the piano, ma'am.” Her work shoe stops against the caster. He turns to the singer who has been standing at the microphone all morning.

**DEAN** — Your piano player never showed. I marked you absent.

**LENA** — I'm right here for my scholarship audition.

Lena holds out her phone. A few notes from the practice record play through its tiny speaker. She looks up.

**LENA** — I practiced with this record.

June recognizes the music.

**JUNE** — I played the piano on that record.

She lets Lena hear the short tag on the keys. A little space before the answering note. Dean's pencil hovers over the attendance sheet.

**LENA** — You saw me sign in.

## 00:44–01:54 · Play it for me

**JUNE** — Want me to play for you?

**LENA** — Could you play the part before the singing starts?

From the small phone speaker comes the introduction. June sits down.

**JUNE** — Listen to the space before the last note.

She plays it. This time the sound fills the hall.

**LENA** — My mom played that record every night.

**DEAN** — That was you? On the original record?

**JUNE** — Ten dollars and a bus ride home.

Her eyes go straight back to the young woman at the microphone. That is who the piano is here for.

## 01:54–03:03 · One verse

A red pencil comes to rest alongside the switched-off recorder. Dean puts real terms to the morning.

**DEAN** — The judges want one continuous take. It has to be in by noon.

**LENA** — Without that scholarship, I can't take the place they offered me.

**JUNE** — Give us one verse before you roll this piano away.

The bench scrapes forward. Dean sets down his pencil.

**DEAN** — One verse. I'm listening.

**LENA** — If I lose my place, just keep going.

**JUNE** — It's your song, Lena. Take the breath you need.

## 03:03–04:03 · The breath

June begins. Lena sings “Keep a light on in the window.” She runs out of air where the recording usually insists on going forward. June takes her hands off the keys. Lena has enough time to decide to sing the answering line herself.

**LENA, singing** — I can find my own way home.

**DEAN** — I heard every word that time.

**JUNE** — Never make a singer chase a piano.

**LENA** — Did you get that take?

## 04:03–05:00 · The red light

The recorder is dark. Dean has heard the whole thing, and the judges will hear none of it.

**DEAN** — I didn't hit record. You're still marked absent.

**LENA** — Then tell the judges I was here.

June folds her hands in her lap. She lets him make the choice in the silence that follows.

**DEAN** — They'll hear whose mistake it was.

He puts a single finger on the record button. The red lamp comes on.

**DEAN** — I marked Lena absent. She was standing right here.

## 05:00–07:10 · What the panel will hear

The light stays red. Dean starts giving the story of the accompanist who didn't come, the two appointments that ran over, the truck scheduled to collect the piano. Lena raises one hand. He stops.

**LENA** — Can I introduce myself before everybody hears what happened to me?

Dean slides the recorder to the edge of the table closest to the stage.

**LENA** — My name is Lena Park. I sing at the counter at Dinah's after we close. Mostly to the guy who mops the floor. He told me to try out for your program.

She cracks up at herself. For the first time she looks her age.

**LENA** — I wrote something much smarter on the bus.

**JUNE** — I'll take the guy with the mop.

Dean checks the wall clock. Thirteen minutes to noon. He has time to make a clean recording. He saves his correction separately and plays it back so Lena can hear her name and his own voice taking responsibility.

**DEAN** — That goes with your submission whichever take you choose.

**LENA** — Thank you. Now can you move that thing somewhere it can hear June too?

## 07:10–09:15 · The bit everyone remembers

They play the next verse. The tune curls toward eight bars of piano that Lena's mother used to tap on the kitchen table. June plays the first two. Lena misses her cue to come back in. She pulls the microphone a little closer and tries again. Same place. Same miss.

**LENA** — You know how many times I listened to that thing to figure out what was wrong with me?

**JUNE** — Nothing wrong with your count. I played the introduction twice.

**LENA** — On purpose?

**JUNE** — Singer dropped her lyric sheet. She was on the floor picking it up. I went round again till I saw her head come back up.

Lena takes her phone out, finds the spot and listens. You can almost feel the young June watching the floor while her hands keep time.

**LENA** — My mother thought it was the cleverest part of the arrangement.

**JUNE** — Smart enough to wait for another person. About as clever as I got that Tuesday.

Lena lays the phone face down. A mistake on an ancient record has been standing between a real singer and the next line of her own audition.

## 09:15–11:15 · Who leads

June asks Lena to walk once from the microphone to the back row. The hall looks different from where Dean was sitting. Lena sees the old photograph inside the lid of her phone case, her mother and two aunts shoulder to shoulder in a narrow kitchen.

**JUNE** — Who are you looking at when you sing that last line?

**LENA** — My mom's back. She used to leave for the night shift before I went to sleep. I'd sing it at the window till she turned around.

**JUNE** — Did she always turn around?

**LENA** — Every time. In the dead of winter she'd take her glove off so I could see her hand.

June waits. Lena gives the first line to the empty chair where she pictures her mother. It comes out an entirely different shape. June doesn't come in until she hears the end of the word “window.”

**LENA** — Let me have the last line alone. When I put my hand up, will you let it go?

June raises her own hand, like a woman turning at the end of a cold street.

**JUNE** — I think I can see that from here.

## 11:15–13:25 · A piano has somewhere else to be

There is a gentle knock at the stage door. The piano dolly has arrived. A clipboard with the instrument number hangs from its handle. The person pushing it waits outside. Dean checks the pickup slip. The truck is exactly on time. That's why June was on the call sheet in the first place.

**DEAN** — I told them they could have it at a quarter to. That was before we lost your pianist.

**LENA** — I didn't lose him. He left me a message to say his kid had a fever. He was trying to do right by somebody too.

Dean looks over the slip. If he sends the truck away there's a second-trip charge to the hall, and his initials belong on that line.

**JUNE** — The small upright in the rehearsal room stays here.

**LENA** — How long to move everybody down there?

**DEAN** — Eleven minutes if I go for the key right now.

The clock says nine minutes. Lena starts collecting her phone and coat. Dean wheels the empty dolly gently backward across the threshold.

**DEAN** — Stay at that microphone. My name's on the rental. I'll have this conversation.

We see him through the square of glass in the stage door. He takes the paper, signs it and passes it back. He has just bought a stranger enough time to finish singing.

## 13:25–15:30 · One continuous take

Lena smooths the creases out of her cardigan and then lets them come back. June moves the bench half an inch because it creaks when she takes her foot off the pedal. Dean comes in carrying nothing. He makes sure the red light is visible to them both.

**LENA** — Can I do the talking this time?

**DEAN** — From here on out.

**LENA** — Lena Park. Singer. June Porter is at the piano. This is “The Window.”

June takes the intro just once. The tune is simple enough to hear the workmanship in it. Lena sings a full first verse and a chorus. Give her the length of a real performance, including the small sounds of turning toward a microphone and taking a breath.

*Keep a light on in the window.*  
*Leave the crooked curtain drawn.*  
*I can see our yellow kitchen*  
*from a block beyond the lawn.*  
*You can take the bus at midnight.*  
*You can leave the porch alone.*  
*Keep a light on in the window.*  
*I can find my own way home.*

The first verse sits easily on June's left hand. For the second, Lena lets a phrase take an extra beat. June hears it and comes with her. Dean has stopped watching the clock. He watches the singer.

## 15:30–17:50 · The missing answer

The part everybody remembers arrives. June plays the first figure and waits to see what Lena wants. In the glass beyond the door a hat disappears as the driver takes the empty dolly away. The piano stays.

Lena raises her hand. June lifts both of hers from the instrument. There is enough silence for Dean to wonder whether something has gone wrong. His finger almost reaches for the recorder. Then he sees the look that passes between the women and draws it back.

Lena sings “I can find my own way home” with no piano under her. It is the quietest sound she has made all morning and the first one that carries to every seat.

Afterward she holds the silence for one beat of her own. Then she puts her hand down.

**LENA** — That's the end.

Dean waits until she says it before stopping the machine. The recorder shows a complete file with the singer's spoken introduction at the top. They listen to the last twenty seconds on its tiny speaker. Halfway through, June mouths the line she didn't play under. She has heard Lena's version only once and already remembers it.

**DEAN** — There's time to have another run if you want that piano back for the end.

**LENA** — That's the one. That's what my mother heard through the glass.

## 17:50–19:45 · Her place

Dean enters the singer's name. Under accompanist, Lena spells out June Porter herself. Dean includes the correction to his attendance sheet and sends the file. Wait with the three people in the real time between clicking send and hearing the confirmation tone.

**DEAN** — Received. Eleven fifty-eight.

Lena lets out an unmusical, enormous breath and laughs.

**LENA** — So did I get it?

**DEAN** — I don't get a vote. You got heard. The people with votes get the rest.

She takes a picture of the time on the confirmation for her mother. The photo includes the upper corner of Dean's navy sleeve holding the recorder still. He is about to apologize for getting in it.

**LENA** — Leave it. You're the one who sent it.

There is another message. The hall coordinator must confirm whether the piano was there for the final applicant. Dean calls the driver back on an ordinary landline. This time he introduces himself, explains and offers the exact pickup time the hall can keep.

## 19:45–21:15 · At the rim of the piano

June comes round to where her work shoe was resting against the caster in the very first shot. She unrolls the protective piano cover. Lena takes the far end. Dean brings the bench clear. The three get it ready for the driver in a little silence after all that talking.

Lena's phone buzzes with a voice message from her mother. There is the sound of a break room, a vending machine and a woman humming the extra introduction from the old record. Then a plain “Call me.”

**LENA** — She'll want to know what it was like singing with you.

**JUNE** — Tell her she raised a singer who can get a piano player to shut up at exactly the right time.

Lena sits on the bench one last time, on the side where the singer can face the pianist. She makes a little room with her hip. June sits beside her to listen while Lena calls her mother. The instrument lid is closed. Their voices are enough.

End credits over the original piano tag and the answering line in Lena's voice.
