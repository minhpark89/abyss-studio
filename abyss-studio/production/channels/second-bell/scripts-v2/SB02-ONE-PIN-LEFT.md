# SB02 · One Pin Left

US English shooting script. Planned running time 20–22 minutes including bowling in real time. The public-facing title is *They Told the Lane Mechanic to Sweep. Her Name Was on the Trophy Three Times.*

Carmen Ortiz, 59, braids her gray hair down her back and wears navy mechanic's coveralls. Tess Miller, 24, bowls in a striped green team shirt and takes her rented shoes back after every match. Reed Daniels, 35, is her opponent in a bright white polo. Deirdre May, 48, runs Ridgeline Lanes and the Saturday singles playoff. This is a local house event. The winner earns its one paid place in the city final. The story runs continuously on lanes seven and eight.

## 00:00–01:30 · The corner pin

Tess's ball skims past the right gutter. The last pin tips, spins on its side and drops out of sight. We hear it land. The scoring screen gives a low buzz. Nine. Reed is on the approach next door. From there he has a better look at Tess's pins than anybody at the desk.

**TESS** — That went down.

**DEIRDRE** — I need ten down to put you through to the last ball.

Carmen comes out of the service door with a push broom. Reed hands her a paper sleeve from his thumb tape.

**REED** — Would you get that, too? We're still bowling here.

She lets the sleeve fall beside his white shoe. Behind his shoulder is a picture of a younger Carmen holding the very cup now on the desk. Same crooked front tooth when she smiles. Deirdre calls to her across the empty approaches.

**DEIRDRE** — Carmen. Three house championships. You ever hear a standing pin make that noise?

**CARMEN** — I heard one fall. Couldn't see whose it was from behind that door.

Tess doesn't ask to see the trophy. She points to the empty spot at the back of her lane.

**TESS** — Can we settle mine before you clear it?

## 01:30–03:10 · Before the sweep

Deirdre pauses the next rack. Everyone can see ten empty pin spots, but the sweep has already passed. Reed sets his ball back down.

**REED** — I had the lead before her turn.

**TESS** — You still get yours. I'm asking for mine.

**DEIRDRE** — I was putting your names on the envelope for the city fee. I didn't watch that ball.

She crosses to Reed. He picks a piece of lint off his shoe instead of looking at her.

**REED** — I turned when the scorer buzzed.

**CARMEN** — Which way?

He gestures vaguely between the two lanes.

**REED** — Can you show us anything better than the screen?

Carmen parks the broom underneath the photo, takes a pair of red rental shoes off the return shelf and sits. A teenager has written GOOD LUCK on the soles in marker. She checks that the shoes are tied. Tess brings her a blue house ball.

**CARMEN** — You're the one making the claim. You pick the ball and you pick where we stand.

## 03:10–05:05 · An honest test

One corner pin stands on lane seven for a demonstration. Let the room watch the whole approach. Carmen puts the ball down a beat later than the younger bowlers. It takes the long way across the lane and sends the pin cleanly sideways. No stomping, no celebration. The display counts it correctly.

**DEIRDRE** — Ten this time.

**REED** — There you go.

For a second it looks as though Carmen has made Tess's case worse. Tess pushes the blue ball to the back of the rack.

**TESS** — Thanks for trying.

**CARMEN** — I said I'd show you a pin falling. I didn't say I could go back two minutes.

She pulls a paper scorecard from the counter. On the back she draws a tiny circle for the corner pin.

**CARMEN** — Somebody did get to see this one. Where were your feet, Reed?

He looks at the two strips of floor between the lanes. The camera stays on his feet. He's still standing in the same place.

## 05:05–07:10 · What Tess paid

Deirdre moves the little trophy table clear of the ball return. Someone beyond the snack counter tentatively begins clapping for Reed and stops. Tess takes a folded white apron out of her bag to find her phone. There's a flour mark on the cheek she wipes with it.

**DEIRDRE** — If you have to get to work, I can call you with the result.

**TESS** — I gave away my Saturday shift in February for this seat. I'll stay in it.

She folds the apron inside out so the flour doesn't get on the upholstery. On her screen is a picture from the bakery. Three coworkers in paper hats are holding bowling poses behind the counter.

**TESS** — They closed the drive-through for three minutes to watch the last frame. Now I have to tell them it's still the last frame.

She records a five-second message. “Still here. Save me an end piece.” The effort to sound ordinary costs her. She puts the phone away.

**CARMEN** — First time you got this far?

**TESS** — First time my Saturdays lined up. I can lose. I just want to know to who.

## 07:10–09:20 · Reed's mark

Reed has his little paper practice book out. He logs every ball himself. Beside the ninth frame he wrote that his thumb stuck. Beside Tess's last throw there is a faint pencil mark. Deirdre reaches for the book. He puts his hand flat on it.

**DEIRDRE** — That's your own card. You don't have to give it to me.

**REED** — My dad kept these. I started writing in the same book the year he quit.

**CARMEN** — Then you'll know why I keep mine.

She takes an old card from behind her picture. Her winning number is faded. On the back, in a different hand, someone has written TAKE YOUR BRACE HOME. She rubs her thumb over the words. It's the only part she can still read without her glasses.

**CARMEN** — My wife had to drive back for that thing every Saturday. Three trophies, and this is what she wanted preserved.

Reed laughs in spite of himself. He opens his father's book, but only to a blank page.

**REED** — You get one place in that city final. I came second four years straight.

**TESS** — Then beat me with your last ball.

## 09:20–11:30 · What a replay can tell them

Deirdre brings up the short spectator video from the snack counter. We see it once. Tess's green sleeve crosses in front of the camera just as the ball reaches the deck. There is the unmistakable sound of wood. A second ball arrives on the next lane almost at the same moment.

**DEIRDRE** — Two balls. One microphone.

**TESS** — I was following mine all the way.

**CARMEN** — I believe you. That phone can tell us where the camera was. Let's quit asking it where you were looking.

Deirdre offers to have Tess throw at the corner once more. She brings the blue ball back herself. Tess sets a hand on it as though she might accept.

**TESS** — If I miss the next one, does that make the first pin stand back up?

Deirdre takes the ball away. The suggestion sounded fair until she heard it said aloud.

**DEIRDRE** — No. And if you make it, I've given you two turns. Forget I asked.

The group stops looking at machines and phones. Reed has opened his book to the right page.

## 11:30–13:40 · The only witness

He puts the book on the ball return for Tess. There is a spare mark beside her name. He made it before the scoring screen buzzed.

**REED** — It went left after it hit the wall. Landed flat. That's why I put the slash down.

**TESS** — You saw all that?

**REED** — Yes.

**TESS** — And you handed her your trash.

The little white paper sleeve is still by his shoe. He picks it up. For a moment there's nowhere to put it, so he keeps it in his closed hand.

**REED** — When the machine gave you nine, I thought maybe I'd watched the wrong lane. Then you asked me straight and I knew exactly which one I'd watched.

**DEIRDRE** — Why did you let her stand here?

**REED** — Because when it said nine I won. I liked what that felt like for about four seconds. It took me a lot longer than four seconds to say this.

Carmen doesn't supply a kinder explanation. She slides the official card toward him. He puts down the same spare mark, then prints his full name beside it.

## 13:40–15:30 · A count in plain sight

Deirdre corrects the house score. Both competitors still have a ball to throw. She puts two paper cards side by side at the desk so anybody on either approach can see the count. She says out loud what she changed and who witnessed it.

**DEIRDRE** — Tess earned that spare. Reed saw it. The decision is mine. If either of you wants to walk away now, I'll understand.

**TESS** — I've been sitting here waiting to bowl.

Reed offers his hand. Tess looks at it, then at the paper sleeve crumpled in it. He throws the paper away.

**TESS** — After the last ball. Like always.

The room settles. Carmen has started to take off the rental shoes. Tess asks her to keep them on.

**TESS** — When I look round, I'd like to see somebody down here who's actually played this part.

Carmen sits behind the foul line, far enough back to leave Tess the whole approach. She tucks both loose laces into her shoes.

## 15:30–18:05 · Two deliveries

These are the only two deliveries that count. Play each from the instant the bowler picks up the ball until the pins finish moving. Tess rolls first. Nine fall. The stubborn corner pin rocks twice, then stands perfectly still. From the snackbar comes a groan. Tess gets close enough to see daylight under neither side of it. She marks nine herself.

**TESS** — It gets to stay up this time.

Her coworkers send a video of the bakery line leaning nine bodies to the left. She watches it once and puts the phone face down so she can give Reed what he failed to give her. She watches his ball from the start.

He needs all ten for the win. Halfway into his approach he stops and steps back. His hand is damp. No one hurries him. His second approach is smaller and better. The corner pin is the last to fall. For once, everyone in the room is looking exactly where they should be.

**DEIRDRE** — By one.

Tess marks ten on Reed's card. Now she holds out her hand.

**TESS** — You got me.

He shakes. It costs her something. Let us see that too.

## 18:05–20:30 · The picture they keep

Deirdre gives Reed the city entry envelope. He takes it. Tess returns her rental shoes. Carmen asks her to save the blue ball from the rack while she fetches the camera. There will be one official winner's photo. Reed stands by the trophy, then notices that Tess is out of the frame.

**REED** — I'd like her in it.

**TESS** — That's yours. Hold it up properly.

He does. She takes a good clean picture of him holding it, the way she would have wanted him to take hers.

Then Deirdre puts the two signed scorecards on the tabletop, the old trophy photo above them. Tess takes a second picture. That is the picture she sends to the bakery. The reply arrives before she can pocket her phone. WE SAVED THE END PIECE.

At the door she sees Carmen back in her work shoes, ready to push the broom. Tess picks up a small scrap of tape from her own shoe and puts it in the bin herself.

**TESS** — Next Saturday, do you work or bowl?

**CARMEN** — At nine I work. At ten I bowl.

**TESS** — I'll bring breakfast.

On the practice lane, one last corner pin is standing. Carmen lays the ball into Tess's waiting hands. Cut on Tess taking the weight. End credits over the sound of a real ball traveling the length of the lane.
