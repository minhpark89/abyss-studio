# SB04 · Next Five

A Second Bell Pictures screenplay. US English. A Saturday morning at the Mercer Street recreation center in Michigan. Target for the full film is 20 minutes, including the games played in continuous action. The review excerpt covers the first encounter.

**VAL MERCER, 59**, a former professional wheelchair basketball guard. A dry alto. She brought water for one game. There is a clean work shirt folded beneath her seat.

**ELI PRICE, 25**, a Saturday captain in a burgundy tee. He has the cabinet key and the only dry-erase pen in the gym. He is good at bringing people together and so used to doing it that he thinks he owns the list.

**NATE BOWEN, 26**, Eli's oldest friend. He tends the breakfast grill down the street. He shows up with the lunch special still written on the heel of his hand.

**JO PRICE, 53**, Eli's mother. She used to take the same bus as Val after games. She works the kitchen at the rec center on Saturdays. Three regular players and the departing five complete the gym.

## 1. The blue line · Interior, gym, morning

A man's hand sends somebody toward the bottom row of bleachers. The ball under his other arm makes a little sucking sound against his damp shirt.

**ELI**  
Spectators sit behind the blue line.

The woman he is directing has the thin black tires of her sports chair straddling that very line. Val looks behind her. There is nobody else there.

**VAL**  
I put my name down to play.

He glances at the board on the scorer's table. Four names in blue. A fifth, VAL, in green. His thumb is already in position to wipe it off. The guys still playing on the other half of the court call for a new ball. He pretends that's what he turned around for.

Val opens a hand.

**ELI**  
From there?

He sends her a bounce pass. She takes the weight of the ball on her lap. One bounce. She runs her thumb across a bald patch on the leather, finds a seam, sets her elbow, releases. Keep her right hand in the air until the sound comes back.

The ball goes straight through. A regular stoops to collect the rebound and finds that it is already in his hands. Eli finally looks at Val's face.

**ELI**  
My mom had your jersey.

**VAL**  
You still need a fifth?

He still has his thumb on the board. Take us out of the excerpt here. In the full film, the game on the far end stops. Five players waiting to check in look at Eli. One of them is Val.

## 2. The name in green

The white board has two columns. ON and NEXT. A little stack of damp paper towels lives beside it. Eli always announces the next five by name. Today he gets through four, sticks the pen in his mouth and jogs to the door. Nobody follows.

**VAL**  
He's gone to count us from outside.

One of the regulars, DEE, laughs so hard she lets her ball get away. Val stops it under her palm and passes it back without looking.

At the door, Eli can see all the way down to the neighborhood diner. No sign of Nate. A new message on his phone says FOUR MINUTES. Right above it is the breakfast special Nate texted him an hour ago. He goes back in.

**ELI**  
My guy's on his way. I gave him my word.

**VAL**  
Which one of these is your name?

He touches the first line. Val reads ELI. Then she taps the green name at the bottom.

**VAL**  
Okay. So we both know which spot you had to give him.

Dee turns the scoreboard from eleven back to zero. The losing team hasn't left the floor. If the next five aren't ready, the losers will take another game. They are already matching up.

Eli hands the ball to Val. His fingers stay on it just long enough for her to wonder whether he's about to give her a speech. He lets go.

**ELI**  
Shirts against darks. We got darks.

**VAL**  
That a position?

**ELI**  
What do you play?

She sends the ball back hard enough to make him take one step.

**VAL**  
You'll figure it out.

## 3. First trip down

Let the first three possessions play at ordinary speed. Eli takes the ball up on the right. Val trails on the left with room to shoot. He sees her, waves for her to come closer, and passes it to Dee in traffic. Turnover. The regulars slap their legs and get back. Val counts the lane lines under her breath.

On the second trip Eli very carefully rolls the ball to Val. The slow pass lets her defender get a hand on it. The ball disappears beneath the bleachers. Everyone has to wait while Eli lies flat on his stomach to fish it out. There's a stale pretzel under there. It sticks to his forearm.

**VAL**  
You always pass that soft?

**ELI**  
I didn't want to hit you in the face.

**VAL**  
Well, you nearly hit me in the birthday.

He looks at her.

**VAL**  
That's how long I was waiting.

She pulls the pretzel off his sleeve. The ice goes out of his shoulders. Next possession he throws a clean chest pass to the place where she is going to be. Val catches in stride, draws the left defender and sends it straight back to him. An easy layup. The scoreboard flips to one. For the first time this morning, they look like a team.

At the kitchen hatch Jo Price, apron on, slides a tray of warm rolls onto the counter. She can see the entire left wing through the hatch. She knows who made the pass before she sees her face.

**JO**  
Somebody get Val a towel. She takes the paint off that side.

Val looks for the voice. Jo raises a blue dish towel like a flag. Val wheels to the hatch during the water break. Two palms meet over the counter.

## 4. The number on the shirt

Eli's phone lights up. He lets it ring and asks Val a question he has apparently had ready since he was nine.

**ELI**  
Was that you with thirty-one in the final? My mom says you could hit from the parking lot.

**VAL**  
From the parking lot I can usually hit my unlock button.

Jo has gone into the little office. She comes back with a folded white jersey. It has been washed so many times that the V in MERCER has opened down the middle. There are two square holes where Jo cut the laundry tag out. It made infant Eli scratch at his cheek when he fell asleep against her.

**JO**  
Show the back to the camera and I'll put it away again.

Eli pockets his phone. Val examines the hem. A set of small brown stitches still holds at one side.

**VAL**  
You fixed that in a laundromat with dental floss.

**JO**  
Your uniform budget was six dollars and a sandwich.

Eli thought this was a souvenir his mother bought. She tells him that when he was a baby, she kept score for the exhibition games. At the last game the folding scorer's table collapsed on her coat and tore the sleeve clean off. Val took off the jersey, pulled it over baby Eli and told Jo to bring it back Saturday.

**ELI**  
How long ago was that?

**VAL**  
How old are you?

The clock on the far court sounds for water. Val takes a long drink. The jersey goes back to Jo. No autograph. Jo doesn't have to ask.

**ELI**  
You ever miss playing in front of people?

**VAL**  
I'm trying to play in front of you right now.

She is already headed down court. Eli goes too. Behind them, Nate reaches the double doors, still tying one shoe.

## 5. A promise at the grill

Nate changed shirts in the diner walk-in. He forgot to take the apron off first. Part of its white strap is sticking out from his shorts. He sees Val dribbling in what he thinks is his spot. He looks up at the board. He waits for a dead ball before he speaks.

**NATE**  
You said you'd hold it.

**ELI**  
I said I'd get you in.

**NATE**  
No. You said hold. I remember because the eggs were burning while you were typing.

Nate shows him the text. FIFTH SPOT'S YOURS. GO FINISH UP. There are two little thumbs-up underneath it, both from Eli. Nate had offered to leave the grill at eight. Eli told him he had time to wash down the breakfast shift first. Nate has forty minutes till he has to open for lunch.

Dee pulls the scoreboard chain twice. A dead bulb flickers. Val could settle this by saying she only wanted one shot. She looks at her folded work shirt. Her own shift starts at eleven. She wants the rest of her game.

**VAL**  
What did you tell him when he was standing in front of you?

**ELI**  
He was at the diner.

**VAL**  
I'm the one standing in front of you.

She lets him sit with it. The gym is quiet enough to hear somebody's ice rolling around in a bottle.

**NATE**  
I don't want your seat, ma'am.

**VAL**  
Good. This one was fitted.

That's for Nate, and Nate takes it as intended. He squats beside the scorer's table to tie the other shoe.

**NATE**  
I want what he promised me.

**VAL**  
Me too.

## 6. Eli takes the towel

Eli uncaps the pen. At first he means to move Val onto NEXT. His hand hovers over the green letters. Then he erases his own name from ON and puts NATE in the empty space. He writes ELI first under NEXT. He takes the towel from his shoulder and sets it on the scorer's chair.

**ELI**  
Nate, you're in. Val runs point. I got the board.

He gives Nate the ball without a show. Two players have already started to clap, then think better of it. Nate mouths thank you. Eli taps the face of his watch. They have time for one good game if everyone quits congratulating him.

The first few minutes at the table are agony. From there Eli sees every late rotation. He can't stop calling plays. Val rolls the ball to the sideline and waits.

**VAL**  
You want to trade places again?

**ELI**  
No.

**VAL**  
I can't hear Nate over there when you're coaching from over here.

Eli puts one end of the towel between his teeth. Val puts the ball back in. Jo slides him a breakfast sandwich in wax paper. He hands it to the kid keeping the other team's score. Without something for his mouth to do, he will start talking again.

Now let the game run. Nate knows how to get into the lane but keeps leaving the place where Val expects him. She taps the maple twice with her fingertips at the same spot. On the third trip he stays there. Val turns a bad pass into a bounce through the narrow gap. Nate catches. Bucket. When he runs past the table, Eli silently holds up two fingers, their old signal. Nate touches two fingers to his ribs.

## 7. Game point

Ten all. Next basket wins. Nate is bent at the waist and Val's arms shine with sweat. A defender leaves Nate to stay on Val. The whole building saw her first shot. Everybody expects a repeat.

Dee checks the ball in to Val at the same blue stripe. Same camera height as the opening. Let the ball sit on Val's palm. Someone in the back says, almost under their breath, “From there?” Eli looks to see who said it. It was him.

Val brings the ball up to shoot. Her defender rises. Val sends the ball along the floor instead. Nate, alone under the basket, has looked away. The ball goes right between his shoes. The others take off down court. Nate catches up with them. He keeps his feet still, gets two hands on the rebound and hangs on.

**NATE**  
I thought you were taking it.

**VAL**  
I thought you wanted to play.

No timeout. Just the two of them getting back in place while everyone else picks up a check. This time the defender holds. Val has her shot. She takes it. The ball hits the inside iron, crosses to the other side and rolls off. Stay with the miss. Nate boxes out for her rebound. She gets one hand on it, knocks it to Dee, who could shoot but sees Nate going back to his spot.

The same three people touch the ball again in a different order. Dee to Val. Val to Nate. This time he is looking. Short bank shot. Game.

At the table Eli puts up eleven. Then he looks down. He had been so busy watching the pass that he has written the winning point in the wrong column. Both sets of regulars are looking at him.

**ELI**  
Give me a second. I know whose that is.

He fixes the score. The winners line up on the blue stripe, breathing hard. Val gets exactly the same sweaty handshake as everybody else.

## 8. Next five

Nate has seven minutes to get back to the grill. He puts Eli's copy of the cabinet key on the scorer's table.

**NATE**  
Thanks for saving me a place.

Eli starts to answer. Nate is looking straight at Val.

**VAL**  
Thanks for looking up.

Eli folds the towel in half and straightens the two columns. He starts reading NEXT out loud. The first person on it is himself. It feels a little strange saying his own name after a morning of calling other people's.

Val is pulling on the clean shirt. Jo offers her the old jersey. The offer is careful. It would look good in a glass case, and they both know it.

**VAL**  
Put it in the wash first. Your kid's had breakfast on it for twenty-five years.

**JO**  
I'll bring it back Saturday.

**VAL**  
You said that last time.

They laugh without stopping what their hands are doing. Jo slides the jersey back into the cupboard above the measuring cups. Val waves once at Nate through the wired glass in the swing door.

A stranger is waiting with a pen in hand. He asks Eli which names are the regulars. Eli turns the board round so the stranger can reach it.

**ELI**  
You write yours. We read down.

The marker squeaks. From the doorway Val hears it, then hears a ball hit wood. She returns the pen she borrowed that morning to the ledge. The only green name on the board is hers. By next Saturday there will be more.

END.
