# SB03 · Room at the Table

US English shooting script. Planned running time 21–23 minutes, including fittings and a walk across the shop in real time. The public-facing title is *The Bridal Shop Sent the Presser to the Back. He Built the Famous Gown in Their Window.*

Ben Cruz, 64, Filipino American, wears a white shop coat over a denim work shirt. He presses suits at Hollis Bridal and used to cut costumes for a touring theater. Talia Brooks, 30, a Black architect, is getting married tomorrow at city hall. Her younger sister Mae, 26, has brought breakfast. Paige Hollis, 42, owns the shop and personally promised Talia a finished fitting by lunchtime. Present day. One shop, one morning, continuous action.

## 00:00–01:30 · A chair for the bride

Talia looks spectacular standing on the fitting platform in her mother's ivory dress. Paige brings a full-length mirror closer. Mae starts a video for the family. Talia asks for a chair.

She tries to sit. Halfway down the back of the dress pulls tight. Talia catches the open chair with one hand, steadying herself. Mae quietly stops filming.

**TALIA** — I can stand up and get married in it. Where do you want me to eat?

Ben is passing with a garment bag. He sets it down and brings a second chair with a lower back. Talia looks at him in the mirror.

**PAIGE** — Ben, I need the suits pressed. Let me handle my bride.

Behind him in the street window stands a scarlet theater gown. In the accompanying opening-night photograph, a younger Ben is on his knees sewing that very hem. An actress in full makeup is holding his cup of coffee. Mae notices that the two men have the same dimple.

**MAE** — Is that you in the window?

**BEN** — Tuesday night in Milwaukee. Coldest coffee I ever drank.

**TALIA** — You made that red dress?

**BEN** — Everything the audience couldn't see. May I look at what yours does when you sit?

He waits for her answer. She steps off the platform herself.

## 01:30–03:20 · Tomorrow's lunch

Paige opens the fitting-room curtain and gives Talia a cotton robe. Mae takes the family dress across both arms. It's soft at the elbows where their mother must have leaned on something.

**TALIA** — We're eating lunch at her kitchen table after the ceremony. A photographer gets ten minutes. My family gets the whole afternoon.

**MAE** — Twelve people at a table that seats eight.

**TALIA** — We can manage the table. Can you help me sit at it?

Ben opens a drawer of plain cotton sample jackets and fitting shells. He hands a loose sample to Paige, who helps Talia into it behind the curtain. Talia comes out wearing the white sample over her ordinary trousers.

Paige puts the ivory dress safely on a hanger. She has a ready-to-wear gown that would fit Talia today. It's already pressed and would need a short hem. She gives that option to Talia without trying to put it on her.

**PAIGE** — If you want certainty at eleven this morning, that one can give it to you.

**TALIA** — I made my sister a promise about this dress. I'll hear him out first.

## 03:20–05:00 · The first fit

Ben rolls the sample chair in front of the mirror. Watch his hands during one complete trial. He frees the back of the muslin fitting shell and adds a folded scrap of cotton between its edges. The original dress stays on the hanger the whole time. He steps away and puts his hands in his pockets.

**BEN** — Try reaching for something on the table.

Mae holds the paper breakfast bag across the chair. Talia sits. She can reach all the way to the bag, fish out a doughnut and rise again without touching the back of the chair. She breaks the doughnut in half and gives Mae the good half with the extra glaze.

**PAIGE** — That panel is going to show.

**TALIA** — I can live with a seam being visible. Can you take it out again on Monday?

**BEN** — Out of the sample? In my sleep. Out of your mother's silk? I'd have to look at how she closed it.

Mae puts her breakfast down. She's waiting harder for that answer than the bride.

## 05:00–07:10 · Two names in the bag

Mae unfolds the old garment bag. Inside its flap, stitched in ordinary blue thread, are the two sisters' first names. One is tall and careful. The second runs uphill and almost off the edge.

**MAE** — I was six. She let me do the M by myself.

**TALIA** — Mom used to say it was the only thing in the house we never fought over.

**MAE** — Because I wasn't allowed to touch it.

They laugh, but Mae folds the flap back over the names. Ben sets the chair so she can see the back of the dress too.

**TALIA** — I get to wear it first. It goes back in the bag just as she left it, for Mae. That's the deal.

**PAIGE** — I wish I'd known that at the first fitting.

**TALIA** — You asked if I wanted to take it in or let it out. I didn't know there was a third answer.

Ben turns the garment cover inside out so the silk sits on clean cotton. He asks Mae to stay with him while he looks. This is her fitting too.

## 07:10–09:20 · The wrong white

Paige comes back with three pieces of new ivory satin. She lays them against the old dress near the street window. Every piece looks almost right alone. Beside the family silk, every piece looks wrong. She releases the stiffest sample and it springs back like a card.

**MAE** — That one would hold its own piece of cake.

Ben turns over the softest sample. The dull side is nicer next to the old silk. He drapes it over the cotton shell and asks Talia to walk from the window to the counter, sit down, cross her ankles, then get up to greet someone. Play the walk without a cut. A little bulge shows on the last turn. Ben sees it before she points.

**BEN** — I got you down into the chair. I've still got to get you out to hug your aunt.

**PAIGE** — We have two hours until the theater wants its display gown back and the appointment after yours is at noon.

**TALIA** — If this is going to take longer than you have, tell me while I can still change my mind.

Paige checks her calendar. She cancels her own delivery lunch, pulls up a chair and picks up the chalk.

**PAIGE** — For the next hour, you have both of us.

## 09:20–11:35 · The dress in the window

The empty window stand gives Paige an idea. She and Ben lift down the scarlet theater gown to pack it for pickup. Talia has always seen it from the street. Ben shows her the ordinary faded inside, with tiny marks left by actors finding their places in the dark.

**BEN** — The song was forty seconds long. She had to come off as a widow and go back on as a woman who'd made up her mind. If the costume caught, you could hear the orchestra starting the bridge again.

**MAE** — Did it ever catch?

**BEN** — In Milwaukee. Coffee down the front. She missed the bridge and walked back on laughing. Best night in the show's life as far as I'm concerned.

Paige holds up a faded, detachable fold from the stage dress. It has carried the weight of a full skirt, night after night. Ben lets her see how the plain inner part lies against his hand when he bends his wrist. Nothing is taken from the theater garment. He packs it intact.

Paige returns to her new fabric. She changes the direction of the fold instead of adding more cloth. Talia tries the muslin again. This time the bulge disappears on the walk back.

## 11:35–13:45 · What the bride really promised

Mae has her coat on. Talia notices that she is standing by the front door, holding the empty family bag.

**TALIA** — Did I push you into saying yes?

**MAE** — You keep promising to put it back exactly the way it was for me. Have you ever asked me if I want to get married in it?

Talia looks at the stitched names. Her own is the careful one.

**MAE** — I want it back because we both own it. I might wear it. I might spend the rest of my life eating pizza with the same woman and never buy a ring. I don't want that to mean I broke my half of the promise.

**TALIA** — You helped me pick the flowers. You took this morning off. I thought you wanted your turn at all of it.

**MAE** — I wanted my turn at breakfast with you.

There is half a doughnut on the counter with a tiny crescent missing. Talia sits on an ordinary chair in her sample dress and pulls a second chair beside her. She and her sister eat the rest in the shop window while Paige and Ben work at the back.

**TALIA** — Tomorrow, if the table's tight, you get the chair next to mine.

**MAE** — I always do.

## 13:45–16:00 · Paige puts down her shears

On the worktable is the finished strip of new silk with its soft inner fold. The old dress has a row of tiny hooks at the back. The bottom two are farther apart than Paige remembers. For a moment the cleanest line would be to cut a single notch in the old seam.

She picks up her shears. Looks over at the sisters sharing the chair arm in the window. Puts the shears down. She brings the dress to them so they can see the choice before anyone makes it.

**PAIGE** — I can give you a back that looks like one piece from across the room. I'd have to open the old silk. Or I can leave these hooks where your mother put them. You will see a narrow line on either side of the new piece.

**TALIA** — Can I see those two lines when I'm eating lunch?

**PAIGE** — No.

**TALIA** — Then let the photographer worry about them.

Mae runs the back of her finger along the original hooks once. Ben asks her to make the last fastening of the trial herself. When she parts it again, the added piece comes away whole in her hand. The old dress lies in front of her exactly as she brought it in. She finally puts down the garment bag.

## 16:00–18:25 · Room

Talia steps into the family dress. Paige closes the old hooks against the new panel. Ben stays outside the curtain until Talia calls him in. The camera follows the fabric as Talia comes off the platform and crosses the shop, a whole walk with no cut to hide a bad fit.

Mae has made a lunch table out of the suit counter. Two paper cups and one last crumpled napkin. She puts the chair where a real person would use it, slightly too close to the table. Talia nudges it back with her calf, sits, turns sideways and reaches over to hug her sister.

The back of the dress moves with her. Talia stays in the hug longer than the demonstration needs. Paige, watching in the mirror, lowers her tape measure. She has been holding her breath.

**BEN** — Can you get back up by yourself?

Talia gets up carrying both empty cups. She sets them beside him, takes three little dance steps with Mae, and sits again. This time she stays seated.

**TALIA** — Tomorrow we are absolutely getting gravy on this.

**MAE** — We can start worrying about that on Monday.

## 18:25–20:10 · The label

Paige writes up the bill at the lunch table. She explains which piece is new and that Talia and Mae can bring the dress back after the honeymoon to remove it. Talia asks for Ben's name on the alteration ticket, and Paige reaches for an old preprinted form.

**BEN** — Put her name on there too. Look at that fold. Mine stuck out like a coat pocket. She turned it.

Paige turns over the form and writes on the blank back. Two names for the work, two names for the owners. Mae asks if they can keep the muslin shell. She puts it on over her sweater. It looks ridiculous, and she is delighted.

**MAE** — I might make something my own size with this.

**BEN** — Come by Thursday. We can put pockets in it.

She raises her eyebrows at Talia. A pocket in her mother's old dress would have caused an international incident. An unlimited number of pockets in her own cotton shell is suddenly a wonderful prospect.

## 20:10–21:45 · The next appointment

An unseen customer rings the front bell. Paige puts the stage gown in its case for pickup. In the empty display window she places the fitting chair from the workroom. Ben starts to carry it back, out of long habit. Paige takes the seat cushion and squares it herself.

**PAIGE** — Leave this one here. I want the next bride to try it.

Mae hangs the family dress in its bag, with the new panel wrapped separately for the drive home. Talia carries the doughnut bag and the bill. At the door she turns back.

**TALIA** — You two have lunch plans?

Ben looks at the cold coffee he forgot at the start of the morning. Paige takes it to the sink without asking.

**BEN** — I know a table that seats eight.

Outside, the sisters walk away with the garment bag between them, each holding one handle. Behind the glass Paige is already listening to the next customer sitting in the chair. End credits.
